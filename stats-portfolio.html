Statistical Methods

Statistics provides the tools to turn observations into meaningful evidence. Through this course, I have explored descriptive and inferential statistics to understand how data can be collected, analysed and interpreted.

What I Have Learnt

Key concepts from the course and how they contribute to my understanding of data.

01

Understanding and Describing Data

I learnt about the measures of central tendency and the measures of dispersion, and how they help describe large datasets. I also learnt how to represent data with different types of graphs, such as scatter plots and histograms. To select the appropriate graph type, it is important to understand what the data represents. Correct representation makes data easy to interpret.

Measures of central tendency Measures of dispersion Data visualization
02

Probability

I learnt concepts such as conditional probability, Bayes' theorem, and addition and multiplication rules. Understanding different types of probability distributions, and constructing Probabibility Mass Functions and Probability Density Functions which provide ways to estimate the likelihood of events.

Conditional probability Bayes' Theorem Probability distribution
03

Sampling methods

I understood that various sampling methods, such as random, systematic, stratified and cluster sampling offer different ways of selecting representative observations from a large population, since it is not always practical study an entire population. I also learnt why sampling bias, sampling error and appropriate sample size matter when drawing conclusions from field data.

Sampling methods
05

Estimation and Confidence Intervals

Estimation showed me how sample data can be used to understand a larger population. Point estimates provide single values, while confidence intervals communicate uncertainty around an estimate. Standard errors help describe the variability of sample estimates.

Point estimation Confidence intervals
06

Hypothesis Testing

Hypothesis testing introduced me to a systematic approach of evaluating claims using sample data. I learnt how to formulate null and alternative hypotheses, select an appropriate test and interpret the p-value to determine whether the observed evidence is inconsistent with the null hypothesis.

Null hypothesis P-value Significance level
08

Correlation and Regression

Correlation and simple linear regression helped me understand ways of examining relationships between numerical variables. Pearson's correlation coefficient describes the direction and strength of a linear relationship, and regression models indicate the relationship between variables and can support predictions. I also realised that correlation alone does not establish causation.

Pearson correlation Linear regression Prediction

Overall Reflection

From numerical calculations to evidence-based interpretation.

Learning to Interpret, Not Just Calculate

This course has helped me appreciate that statistics is not limited to performing calculations or applying formulas. It is a way of approaching questions systematically, evaluating evidence and communicating findings clearly. Understanding the assumptions behind a method is just as important as obtaining the numerical result.

I hope to apply these concepts in future geology and geoinformatics projects, particularly those involving environmental monitoring, field sampling and satellite imagery. Statistical thinking can help me move beyond describing patterns to evaluating how reliable the evidence is and what conclusions can reasonably be drawn.

As I continue developing my analytical skills, I want to become more confident in choosing suitable tests, interpreting their outputs and presenting data through clear graphs, maps and explanations.
